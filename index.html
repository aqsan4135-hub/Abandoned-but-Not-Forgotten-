Why this project exists

Make space history easier to explore

This interactive learning archive is designed for school-age learners and curious beginners. It connects each machine with three simple questions: What was it? What did it do? What science did it make possible?

01 · The hardware

Discover the landers, rovers, scientific instruments and spacecraft that remain on the Moon, Mars or in space.

02 · The mission

Learn why NASA sent each machine and what job it was designed to perform.

03 · The science

See how observations, experiments and engineering tests expanded human knowledge.

04 · The legacy

Understand why a completed mission can still have lasting scientific and historical value.

NASA data connection

From archive records to learning stories

The NASA Space Science Data Coordinated Archive (NSSDCA) is NASA's permanent archive for space-science mission data. Its Master Catalog helps users find spacecraft records and mission parameters. This project uses selected catalogue records as references and turns mission information into simple learning stories.

How to read the archive

What does “left behind” mean?

Stationary on a world

Some landers, rovers, descent stages and experiments remain where they completed their work.

Still travelling

Some spacecraft completed their planned observations and continue moving through space.

Not all are the same

“Abandoned” is a storytelling idea in this project. It does not mean every object has the same status or condition.

Evidence matters

Mission facts should be checked against the linked NASA archive or mission page.
